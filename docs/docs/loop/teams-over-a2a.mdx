# A team of applications over A2A

Two applications can work as a team. One asks the other while it works,
over the [A2A protocol](../protocols/index.mdx#a2a-agent-to-agent-protocol).
The team says who asks whom, in agentspecs' teams catalogue
([Teams of applications](https://agentspecs.datalayer.tech/agent-teams/applications)):

```yaml
id: sales-and-accounting
entry: sales
supervisor: { name: Sales, app: sales:0.0.1 }
agents:
  - id: sales
    app: sales:0.0.1
    runs_in: browser
    talks_to: [{ member: accounting, over: a2a }]
  - id: accounting
    app: accounting:0.0.1
    runs_in: runtime
```

- **Sales** (`apps/sales.yaml`) is a chat that runs in the person's browser.
  A person asks it for a financial report. It asks Accounting with one tool,
  `ask_accounting`, and hands over what Accounting answered. It never invents
  a figure.
- **Accounting** (`apps/accounting.yaml`) runs on a runtime. It reads the Odoo
  books through the `odoo-accounting` toolset of the Datalayer MCP gateway at
  *Can read*. Its rules read without asking and ask first before anything
  that would change the books. A connection that only reads reaches no tool
  that writes.

## Scenes

A *scene* — agents arranged with their roles, their shared context and
their interactions, as Datalayer's Studio shows them — is a team and
nothing else (LOOP A-04, agentspecs 0.0.60). What the team model says of a
scene:

- **One member is allowed; none is not.** An agent working alone with its
  data is a scene (`month-end-close`, `crop-monitoring`: *one agent, its
  data*). A team with no member is refused when the catalogue loads.
- **Each member names its `role`**, and the team its shared `context`:
  `sharing` (how much of the conversation each member is told: `shared`,
  `isolated`, `own-turns`) and `frames`, the Frames every member works
  under, in order (`TeamContextSpec.frames`).
- **Each interaction names its protocol.** `talks_to … over: a2a` to an
  agent or an application; `over: mcp` to a member that is an MCP server of
  the catalogue (`server`, in place of `ref` and `app`: a system of the
  scene the others reach tool by tool, which asks nobody). A member that
  talks to one not in the scene, a server asked over A2A, an application
  asked over MCP and a scene that enters at a server are each refused in a
  sentence (`TeamLinkSpec.over`, `TeamAgentspec.server`).

The home page's four scenes are in the teams catalogue
(`src/specs/teams/teams.ts`): `sales-and-accounting` (Sales in the browser
asks Accounting on a runtime; Odoo under Accounting), `month-end-close`
(Month-end close alone on a runtime; Odoo), `crop-monitoring` (Crop
monitoring alone on a runtime; NASA Earthdata) and `disaster-assessment`
(Event response in the browser asks Disaster assessment and Change
detection, each on a runtime; Earthdata under each). The MCP servers under
a member are its application's connections (`teamConnectionsOf`), not
restated in the team. What each scene needs is said as setup, not as a
mistake: the five workers behind them are catalogued and not enabled,
`earthdata` is enabled (its three tools classed: the searches read, the
download writes), `odoo-accounting` is not.

## The transcript

What happens in a scene, as lines, in the order it happened (LOOP A-06):

```
12:00:00  You → Sales: Give me the aged receivables as of today, by customer.
12:00:01  Sales → Accounting: Aged receivables as of today, by customer.
12:00:02  Accounting → Odoo: odoo_accounting_aged_balance
12:00:04  Accounting: Three customers owe 12,400 EUR; the oldest is 91 days.
12:00:05  Sales: Accounting says three customers owe 12,400 EUR; the oldest is 91 days.
```

One source, read two ways (`components/teams/sceneTranscript`, pure: spans
or entries in, lines out):

- **Live**, from the OpenTelemetry spans the Agent Inspector reads — the
  page's one tracer, core's `createOtelLiveTracer`, that `useA2ATeam`,
  `traceA2AFetch` and the chats record into. `transcriptOfSpans(spans,
  members)`: an `invoke_agent` span is what the person asked (*You → Sales*,
  for a turn with no parent) and what the agent answered (*Sales: …*, at its
  end); an `a2a` request is who asked whom in what words (*Sales →
  Accounting*, from `a2a.message.text` and `peer.service`) and what came
  back (*Accounting: …*, from `a2a.answer.text` or the last `a2a.message` /
  `a2a.artifact_update` event; *could not answer* when it failed); an
  `execute_tool` span is a call (*Accounting → Odoo: odoo_accounting_aged_balance*,
  the server being the connection the tool is of, `connectionOfTool` over
  the member's `connections`; a tool of no connection is called on *the
  page*, *a skill*, *codemode* or *its runtime* by `datalayer.tool.kind`).
  A tool call under which an A2A request sits (Sales' `ask_accounting`) is
  the asking itself and is not a line of its own; the same words said twice
  (an answer as the request's events, and as the peer's own turn when a
  runtime's spans are read) are one line. An open span is a line still
  happening (`open`, drawn with an ellipsis); a failed one says so.
- **After**, from a finished run's record (`RecordEntry`, as Activity reads
  it): `transcriptOfRecord(entries, member)` — a `turn` is what the person
  asked and what the member answered, a `tool_call` what it called in
  between (the calls are recorded before the turn closes, so the question is
  placed where the turn began), an `output` stands in for the answer when the
  record keeps no conversations.

`lineText(line)` is a line in words, `transcriptText(lines)` the whole with
a time on each — what *Copy as text* copies. The members are named by their
spans' service (`SceneTranscriptMember`: `id`, `name`, `connections`).

**Drawn.** `SceneTranscript` lists the lines (each its time, who and to
whom, the words or the tool) with *Copy as text*. `SceneView` holds a
scene's graph and its transcript behind a *Graph · Transcript* toggle
(Primer's `SegmentedControl`): the graph stays mounted while the transcript
shows. It reads the transcript from the tracer the members record into
(`tracer` and `members`, through `useSceneTranscript`), or takes a finished
run's lines (`lines`):

```tsx
import { A2ATeamGraph, SceneView, teamConnectionsOf } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/components/teams';

<SceneView
  tracer={inspector}
  members={[
    { id: 'sales', name: 'Sales' },
    { id: 'accounting', name: 'Accounting', connections: teamConnectionsOf(ACCOUNTING) },
  ]}
>
  <A2ATeamGraph … />
</SceneView>;
```

The example shows it: the box above the inspectors is a `SceneView`, its
graph as before, its transcript read from the same tracer. Tests: a
scripted scene gives the expected lines (`sceneTranscript.test.tsx`).

## Rehearsal

A scene spec's `rehearsal` (agentspecs `scenes/`, LOOP A-14) says, per
beat, the shape the transcript must take — in the grammar above, `You →
Sales`, `Sales → Accounting`, `Accounting → Odoo: odoo_accounting_*`,
`Accounting: a table`, `Sales: words` — the words the answer must and must
not hold, and the time the beat may take. `loop scenes rehearse <scene>`
plays each beat's cue through the scene and judges it:

- **The stage** (`agent_runtimes.loop.scenes.stage`): the entry's application
  runs in this process as `loop apps run` runs one (`AppHost`, its agent from
  `local_agent` or one a test gives), with a tool per member it talks to,
  `ask_<member>`, declared as a tool of its own that reads. The ask is an A2A
  request: to the member in this process (a session of its own) or, with
  `--cloud`, to the member served over A2A on a cloud runtime of its own, as
  `serve_accounting.py` serves Accounting, asked with a key granted to its
  route (`--key <member>=<key>`), its tool calls read off the working
  statuses of the stream as `askA2APeer` reads them.
- **One grammar**: what happens is recorded as the spans the Agent Inspector
  reads (`invoke_agent`, `execute_tool`, `a2a`, each with the attributes
  above) and read back by `agent_runtimes.loop.scenes.transcript`, the Python
  twin of `sceneTranscript.ts` — `transcript_of_spans`, `transcript_of_record`,
  `line_text`, `transcript_text`. Both are held to one fixture,
  `agent_runtimes/tests/fixtures/scene_transcript.json` (the scripted scene,
  what is still happening and what failed, a tool of no connection, the
  same answer said once), read by `sceneTranscriptFixture.test.ts` and
  `test_scenes_transcript.py`.
- **The verdict** (`agent_runtimes.loop.scenes.rehearsal`): the expected lines
  found in the transcript in order (a member by its id or its shown name, a
  system by its id, server or `as`; a detail as a pattern or words; *a
  table* read off the answer), `must_say` / `must_not_say` on the entry's
  answer, `within` on the time; each beat **passed**, **failed** with what
  differed, or **not run** with why — a member whose setup is incomplete
  leaves the beats that need it not run with its sentence. A scene with a
  recording and no live run says its recording stands. Exit codes are `loop
  apps validate`'s (1 failed, 3 not run); a scene whose every beat passed is
  *Live*.

`loop scenes ls` lists the catalogue: each scene's face, members (where each
runs, whom it asks, what it reaches), beats, setup, and whether it was
verified live. The whole is on the [CLI page](/cli#a-scenes-rehearsal); the
tests are `test_scenes_rehearse.py` (a scripted scene: fake members answering
fixed lines, to a pass, a fail and a not run; the command's words and exit
codes).

## The application asked: fasta2a on the runtime

A runtime serves an application over A2A when it is configured with `a2a`:

```bash
# On Datalayer: a new cloud runtime, or a running one with -r <uid>.
loop apps run agentspecs/agentspecs/apps/accounting.yaml --cloud --a2a --keep
loop apps run agentspecs/agentspecs/apps/accounting.yaml --cloud -r <runtime uid> --a2a --keep
# On a runtime that is already running, such as the examples' server on :8765.
python examples/sales-accounting-a2a/serve_accounting.py --url http://127.0.0.1:8765
# Every member on a runtime of every scene, each on a local runtime of its own.
python examples/sales-accounting-a2a/serve_scenes.py --url http://127.0.0.1:8767
```

A runtime serves one application over A2A at a time: configuring another
replaces it. A scene with several members on a runtime needs a runtime per
member, which is what `serve_scenes.py` does locally (below).

`POST /api/v1/apps/configure` with `"a2a": true` (and `public_url`, the
address callers reach) makes the runtime's agent the application's agent,
with its connections and its rules. It also mounts the agent with fasta2a at
`/api/v1/a2a/agents/<application id>/`, through the runtime's A2A route
(`register_a2a_agent`): its task store and broker are the runtime's protocol
state store, and its worker is the runtime's `A2AWorker`. `loop` prints the
address and the agent card, then waits until Ctrl-C.

The agent card is written from the Appspec: its name, description and
version, and one skill named for it whose examples are its starters. A
request is text. An answer is text, and may come in other formats too — see
[Output formats](#output-formats). fasta2a (2.1 and later) answers A2A
1.0 JSON-RPC (`SendStreamingMessage`, `TASK_STATE_*`, `ROLE_*`) as its card
says. The card also says that a bearer key is needed (`securitySchemes`).
Its face — the Appspec's `emoji`, and its `avatar` when it has one — rides
an extension of the card, `https://datalayer.ai/extensions/face/v1`, not
required; its name stays plain text. `connectA2APeer` reads it as
`peer.face` (`faceOfCard`), and the team draws the peer with it; a client
that draws no face ignores it.

**Who it answers** (`agent_runtimes.loop.apps.a2a.A2AGate`):

- the machine itself, without a token. This covers a developer's browser and
  the local server;
- a key granted to this route: a token exchanged from an IAM task grant whose
  task begins with `a2a:<runtime uid>:<application id>:`, checked with IAM.

- a visitor without an account, when the owner opened the application to
  visitors (next section).

Without a token the answer is 401. A person's own token, or a key granted to
another route, gets 403. The agent card is open to anyone.

**As whom it runs.** A remote caller's run acts with the caller's key and
never with the runtime's own: the key becomes the run's delegated credential
(`datalayer.credential`). The run then reaches the gateway with that key
(`toolsets_for_the_run`). This also applies to a catalogue server that is the
gateway with some of its toolsets, such as `odoo-accounting`, which keeps its
`only=` query.

## Open to visitors

A signed-out visitor holds no key of the owner's. An application served over
A2A can also answer visitors, the way LOOP's visitors' runtime (R-30) answers
an `at:<slug>` address. Its owner says so when it is configured, as
`agent-teams demo deploy` does:

```json
POST /api/v1/apps/configure
{
  "app": { "...": "the Appspec" },
  "a2a": true,
  "public_url": "https://<the runtime's ingress>",
  "visitors": true
}
```

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `visitors` | Served over A2A (`a2a` is required too), it also answers visitors. |
| `visitors_key` | Optional. The owner's key visitors' runs act with: a token from an IAM task grant whose task begins with `a2a:<runtime uid>:<application id>:`, reaching the owner's connections read only (the shape `make_temp_key.py` mints). Unsaid, visitors' runs keep the runtime's own credential. Refused with 422 when it is anything else, or without `visitors`. |

The answer's `a2a` then says `visitors`: the runs a visitor has a day, all
of them together, and what the runs act with.

**Who a visitor is.** A visitor's token from ai-inference
(`POST /anonymous/token {"app": "<application id>", "visitor": "<id>"}`),
which names the visitor and this application by its id. The gate asks
ai-inference who it is (`GET /anonymous/whoami`), so the runtime needs
`DATALAYER_AI_INFERENCE_URL`. A token for another application gets 403, a
spent one 401, and a visitor's token on an application not open to visitors
gets 403.

**What a visitor's run does.** It never acts with the visitor's token. The
gate marks the run a visitor's (`datalayer.visitor` in the message's
metadata, whatever the message said), and the run then only reads, as on the
visitors' runtime: a tool that does more than read, or that the
application's rules would ask about, is refused before it runs. It acts with
`visitors_key` when one was given, else with the runtime's own credential,
the owner's. Either way the application's connection reads only:
Accounting's `odoo-accounting` is at *Can read*. Its model calls are the
runtime's, metered to the owner.

**Limits.** The owner pays for these runs, so the gate counts them. Only a
request that starts a run is counted; reading a task is not.

| Variable | Default | What it limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `AGENT_RUNTIMES_A2A_VISITOR_TURNS` | 3 | Runs one visitor starts in a day (UTC). |
| `AGENT_RUNTIMES_A2A_VISITORS_TURNS_A_DAY` | 100 | Runs all visitors start in a day, together. |

Past either, the answer is 429 with a sentence that says which. The counts
live in the runtime's process: a restart starts them again. The visitor's
own model calls (Sales, in the browser) are ai-inference's to limit: its
anonymous rate and its ceiling for the day.

## Output formats

An application says the formats its answers come in, by media type, words
first: the Appspec's `interface.outputs` (agentspecs 0.0.41). Accounting
answers in Markdown and in a Jupyter notebook:

```yaml
interface:
  outputs:
    - text/markdown
    - application/x-ipynb+json
```

**On the card.** They are its card's `defaultOutputModes` and its skill's
`outputModes` (fasta2a 2.1.1 takes the card's modes from the application);
plain text when the Appspec says none. `defaultInputModes` is `text/plain`.

**Asked for.** A caller names the formats it accepts with each request, in
its configuration: `acceptedOutputModes`. fasta2a hands them to the worker
as the task's `accepted_output_modes`. The `A2AWorker` opens the run's
outputs (`agent_runtimes.output.formats`): the formats both the card and the
caller name, words left out, since words are the answer itself. A caller
that names nothing is answered in words alone.

**Composed.** What is not words is composed by the agent with a tool of its
own, offered only in a run that accepts its format. For a notebook it is
`write_notebook(title, cells)`, each cell `{kind: markdown | code, source}`,
and the run's prompt then says how to write it: what it is in a markdown
cell, the figures it read as a pandas `DataFrame` from literals, the analysis
in code cells (pandas, and a matplotlib chart where one helps), and a
markdown cell explaining each result. The runtime checks the notebook before
it keeps it, and a refused one is written again (`ModelRetry`):

- valid nbformat 4, its code cells not run;
- it runs in the reader's browser offline: code imports only pandas, numpy,
  matplotlib and Python's standard library, names no URL, calls neither
  `open` nor `__import__`, and has no magics or shell commands;
- it holds nothing that looks like a credential, a key or a token;
- at most 60 cells and 200,000 characters.

`write_notebook` touches nothing outside the run, so an application's rules
decide it as reading. A visitor's run, which only reads, writes one too.

**Carried.** The notebook is an A2A artifact beside the text: one data part,
the nbformat document itself, `mediaType: application/x-ipynb+json`, with a
file name. While the agent writes it, the `working` status of the tool call
carries a note, `Writing a notebook…`, then `Notebook written`, and not the
cells, which travel once, in the artifact.

**Received.** In the browser, `askA2APeer(peer, request, { accept })` sends
`acceptedOutputModes` and resolves to `{ answer, artifacts }`. Each artifact
is `{ mediaType, name, filename?, data }`, whatever its format, and the
`answered` event carries them too. `a2aPeerTool({ peer, accept })` describes
the formats the peer gives that the page accepts, and takes an optional
`formats` argument, so that the model asks for a notebook when the person
wants one, or for words alone with `[]`. Left out, the page's `accept`
applies. The model is told that a notebook came, not its content.

`useA2ATeam({ …, accept: NOTEBOOK_AND_WORDS })` keeps the peer's `artifacts`
with its last answer, and its latest `notebook` until another one comes.
`TeamNotebook` draws it, titled, with *Download .ipynb*, on the browser
sandbox: the loop notebook view's own `EphemeralNotebook` bound to
`createBrowserSandboxService`, a JupyterLite Pyodide kernel in the page,
where the reader runs cells, changes them and adds their own. The view,
JupyterLab and Pyodide are loaded only once a notebook is drawn: a page that
shows the team loads none of them before.

The notebook goes **right under the team's graph**, before the composer and
the conversation, in the example and on the landing's home page: the page
names that area with a ref, `expandTarget` on the entry member, and the
graph draws the notebook that runs there, through a portal, as it arrives.
Sales' balloon shows it read-only at the same time (`persona.notebook`,
drawn by `NotebookPreview`: jupyter-react's notebook with `readonly` on
`ServiceManagerLess`, no kernel), with *Open it below to run it* and
*Expand*; a click there scrolls to and focuses the one that runs. Without an
area, *Expand* opens it in a dialog over the page. The
members' balloons say their tools too (`persona.tool`): *Asking
Accounting…*, *Using list_invoices…*.

```tsx
import { A2ATeamGraph, NOTEBOOK_AND_WORDS, useA2ATeam } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/components/teams';

const team = useA2ATeam({ entry, peerApp, peer, inference, accept: NOTEBOOK_AND_WORDS });
const notebookArea = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
// …
<A2ATeamGraph
  entry={{ …, persona: team.entryPersona, expandTarget: notebookArea, notebookTitle: "Accounting's notebook" }}
  …
/>
<div ref={notebookArea} />
```

### Components in answers

The other format is components of the catalog (STUDIO H-02):
`application/json+a2ui`, A2UI's media type. The scenes' members on a runtime
name it after Markdown and the notebook (agentspecs 0.0.64):

```yaml
interface:
  outputs:
    - text/markdown
    - application/x-ipynb+json
    - application/json+a2ui
```

**Composed.** In a run that accepts it the agent has `show_components(title,
sources?, table?, chart?, choice?)`, and its prompt says what fits each:

| Given | Drawn with | What the reader gets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sources: [{title, url?, passage?}]` | `Evidence` | the records or pages the answer rests on, as cards; a card with a public link opens it |
| `table: {columns, rows}` | `Table` | rows that compare, under their columns |
| `chart: {kind, x, y, series?, points}` | `Chart` | a series of numbers, a point per row |
| `choice: {question, options: [{label, does}]}` | `Text`, a `Row` of `Button` | two to four buttons that answer the application |

The runtime builds the nodes, not the model: each is checked against the
catalog's JSON Schema (`component_node`), the values sit in the surface's
data model and the nodes read them by path (`/sources`, `/rows`,
`/points`), and a surface the catalog would not draw — nothing to show, an
empty list, a point without its `x` or `y`, a link that is not a web
address, a choice of one option — is refused with a sentence the model acts
on (`ModelRetry`), twice at most. At most three surfaces per answer. Like
`write_notebook`, `show_components` touches nothing outside the run: the
rules decide it as reading, and a visitor's run shows them too.

**Carried.** Each surface is an artifact beside the text: one data part,
`mediaType: application/json+a2ui`, holding `{surfaceId, catalogId, title,
messages}` — the A2UI v0.9 messages that draw it (`createSurface`,
`updateComponents`, `updateDataModel`), the surface's id `answer-…`. While
it is composed the status says *Showing it…*, then *Shown*.

**Drawn.** `useA2ATeam({ …, accept: COMPONENTS_NOTEBOOK_AND_WORDS })` keeps
every surface the members show in the conversation, in the order they came,
each once, with the member that gave it (`team.surfaces`,
`{ id, giver, artifact }`). `AnswerSurfaces` draws them with the A2UI
surface plugin's `InlineSurface` on Datalayer's catalog, in the page's theme,
each titled with who showed it; A2UI's renderer is loaded only once a
surface is drawn.

**A choice answers the application.** A button pressed is the reader's
answer to the member that showed it: `onPress(surface, pressed)` gets the
option's words and its action (`pressedOf`), and the page asks that member
with them — `askA2APeer(peer, pressed.message, { action: pressed.action })`
puts the action in the message's metadata, `loop.action: {name, payload:
{does}}`. One choice per surface; what came back is said under it (a
press that failed is said, and another button can be pressed).

**A press is a turn of the conversation.** In a team, give `onPress` the
team's `pressAction(surface.giver, pressed)`: the person's line goes into
the entry's history, addressed to that member (`turns`' `member`, the
history's `speaker` *You* and `directedTo`), the member is asked directly
with the action, and its reply lands as an `ask_<peer>` answer does — its
history, its balloon, its link's flow, the team's report, surfaces and
notebook — and in the entry's history as that member's answer. The entry's
model reads both at its next turn. In the transcript the press is the
person's: give the peer's `traceA2AFetch` `pressedBy: PERSON`, and a request
carrying `loop.action` is recorded with *You* as its service — *You →
Accounting: Send the reminders* — while the entry's own requests stay its.

**An approval is honest.** An option whose `does` is more than `read`
(`write`, `send`, `delete`…) is an approval. The agent is told to show an
action it would take but does not take itself that way — the action and
*Not now*. Pressed in a visitor's run, the worker refuses it before any
model runs, in the sentence a visitor's tool call is refused with
(`refused_action`, `visitor_refusal`): *Without an account it only reads:
`Send the reminders` would do more than read, so it asked nobody and did
nothing.* Anywhere else the press is the person's turn, and the
application's rules decide: an action it has no tool for is not done, one
that asks first is asked of a person (Tool Approvals).

```tsx
import { AnswerSurfaces, COMPONENTS_NOTEBOOK_AND_WORDS, useA2ATeam } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/components/teams';

const team = useA2ATeam({ entry, peers, inference, accept: COMPONENTS_NOTEBOOK_AND_WORDS });
// …
<AnswerSurfaces
  surfaces={team.surfaces}
  names={{ accounting: 'Accounting' }}
  onPress={(surface, pressed) => team.pressAction(surface.giver, pressed)}
/>
```

## The application asking: `@a2a-js/sdk` in the browser

`runtimes/browser/a2aPeer` uses the A2A SDK's own client (`@a2a-js/sdk` 1.x),
with no wrapper between them:

```ts
import { connectA2APeer, a2aPeerTool } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/runtimes/browser/a2aPeer'; // not in the barrel

const peer = await connectA2APeer({ url, key }); // reads the agent card
const tools = { ask_accounting: a2aPeerTool({ peer, onEvent }) };
```

`connectA2APeer` reads the card and makes a client from it (`ClientFactory`,
`JsonRpcTransportFactory`). The key goes as a bearer token on every request.
`a2aPeerTool` is a Vercel AI SDK tool: one request in words in, the peer's
answer in words out, with the description written from the card's skill.
`onEvent` is told each step as it happens: `asked`, `working` (with the tool
the peer calls), then `answered` (with what it gave besides words) or
`failed`. `accept` names the formats the page accepts
([Output formats](#output-formats)).

## The team as a graph

`components/teams` draws a team and runs it in a page — a team of two, or a
team of N (LOOP A-08). It is not in the components barrel, because it brings
React Flow:

```tsx
import { A2ATeamGraph, teamConnectionsOf, useA2ATeam } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/components/teams';

// What Accounting reaches, from its Appspec: Odoo, through odoo-accounting.
const ACCOUNTING_CONNECTIONS = teamConnectionsOf(ACCOUNTING);

const team = useA2ATeam({ entry: SALES, peerApp: ACCOUNTING, peer, inference, peerConnections: ACCOUNTING_CONNECTIONS });

<A2ATeamGraph
  entry={{ id: 'sales', name: 'Sales', character: 'paperclip', where: 'in your browser', persona: team.entryPersona }}
  peer={{ id: 'accounting', name: 'Accounting', character: 'wizard', where: 'on a runtime', persona: team.peerPersona, connections: ACCOUNTING_CONNECTIONS }}
  flow={team.flow}
  calls={team.calls}
  connected={peer !== null}
  label={`A2A · ${peer.skill.name}`}
/>;
team.send('Which invoices are still open?');
```

- **`A2ATeamGraph`** is a React Flow graph of the two members and the edge
  between them, and of each member's connections under it. Each
  node is the member's character (`AssistantStage`), with its name and where
  it runs. The edge is still until a message travels on it. It flows from
  Sales to Accounting while Sales asks, and from Accounting to Sales while
  Accounting answers. An arrow points at the receiving end, and the edge
  says *Sales asks Accounting* or *Accounting answers Sales*. With reduced
  motion the edge does not move, but the arrow and the words stay. The graph
  is a picture: nothing is dragged, panned or zoomed, and it scales to its
  box, down to a phone's width. React Flow is MIT; its credit is hidden.
- **A member's connections** are the MCP servers it reaches. Each is a node
  half a member's size under it, with the server's mark (`SpecMark`, from
  the spec's `icon`: `@datalayer/icons-react:odoo`), the system it reaches
  (*Odoo*) and *via MCP*, linked to the member by an edge of its own. That
  edge is still until the member calls one of the connection's tools. Then
  it flows toward the connection for as long as the call runs, with the
  tool's name on it (*list invoices*), and the node pulses. A quick call is
  shown for at least `CALL_SHOWN_MS` (0.9 s). With reduced motion the arrow
  and the words stay and nothing moves.
- **`teamConnectionsOf(app)`** reads the connections from the Appspec: each
  `connections[].server`, then the MCP server's spec for its name, mark and
  tools (the tool names of its `actions`). A tool belongs to a connection
  when it is one of those names, with or without a host's prefix, or begins
  with the server's id as the gateway names its toolsets
  (`odoo_accounting_`).
- **`useA2ATeam`** runs the entry's loop in the page (the Vercel AI SDK's
  `ToolLoopAgent`, its model through ai-inference) with its one tool to the
  peer. It keeps each member's persona (its state and its balloon), the
  conversation, each member's history (`entryHistory`, `peerHistory`), the
  last report, the flow, and the calls the peer's connections are answering
  (`calls`, from `callsAfter`). Given the Agent Inspector's tracer
  (`inspector`), it records the entry's model turns and its call to the peer
  there, as OpenTelemetry spans.
- **`flowAfter`** turns an `A2APeerEvent` into the flow. `asked` and
  `working` give `asking`. `answered` gives `answering` for
  `ANSWER_SHOWN_MS` (1.8 s). `failed` gives `still`.

### A team of N

Disaster assessment is three members: Event response in the browser asks
Disaster assessment and Change detection, each on a runtime, each on
Earthdata. The same hook and the same graph run it:

```tsx
const team = useA2ATeam({
  entry: EVENT_RESPONSE,
  peers: [
    { app: DISASTER_ASSESSMENT, peer: assessor, connections: teamConnectionsOf(DISASTER_ASSESSMENT) },
    { app: CHANGE_DETECTION, peer: detector, connections: teamConnectionsOf(CHANGE_DETECTION) },
  ],
  inference,
});

<A2ATeamGraph
  entry={{ id: 'event-response', name: 'Event response', character: 'paperclip', where: 'in your browser', persona: team.entryPersona }}
  peers={team.peers.map(peer => ({ id: peer.id, name: peer.app.name, character: 'wizard', where: 'on a runtime', persona: peer.persona, connections: peer.connections }))}
  flows={team.flows}
  calls={team.calls}
  connected={{ 'disaster-assessment': assessor !== null, 'change-detection': detector !== null }}
  links={teamLinksOf(DISASTER_ASSESSMENT_TEAM)}
  positions={DISASTER_ASSESSMENT_SCENE.stage.positions}
/>;
```

- **`useA2ATeam`'s `peers`** names the members the entry asks, in order.
  The entry gets one tool per peer, `ask_<peer id>` (`askToolOf`), and its
  instructions are followed by a line naming each — *ask Disaster assessment
  with `ask_disaster_assessment`, Change detection with
  `ask_change_detection`* (`teamInstructions`; a team of two keeps the
  entry's instructions as they are). The team is `ready` once every peer is
  reached. Each peer is kept on `team.peers` — its persona, its history, its
  report and artifacts, its flow, `setAway` — and `team.flows` says which
  way each link carries a message, by the peer's id. The calls to each
  peer's connections are its own: one peer's request does not end
  another's call. `peerApp`, `peer`, `askTool` and `peerConnections` still
  name a team of two, and `peerPersona`, `peerHistory`, `flow` are the
  first peer's. A peer may greet before it is asked (`greeting`: a scene's
  `opens_first`). What a member says when asked follows what it reaches:
  *Let me read the books* on Odoo, *Let me look in Earthdata* otherwise
  (`askedLine`).
- **An entry on a runtime** (`entryPeer`: Month-end close, Crop monitoring,
  the scenes of one member) is asked over A2A from the page as the person,
  with no agent in the browser; its persona follows what it tells, as a
  peer's does.
- **`A2ATeamGraph`'s `peers`** draws the entry and N peers, each with its
  MCP connections under it, its balloon, its menu and its inspector;
  `flows`, `connected` and `labels` are per peer id. The edges are the
  team's `talks_to` over A2A (`links`, `teamLinksOf(team)`): a peer that
  talks to another peer has its edge drawn too, still — the page runs the
  entry's asks only. Without `positions`, the entry stands on the left and
  the peers to its right one under another; with a scene's
  `stage.positions` (fractions of the box), the distinct `x` values are the
  columns and the distinct `y` values the rows, so Disaster assessment is a
  triangle: Event response above, the two specialists below it
  (`placeMembers`). An edge leaves and reaches a member by the side that
  faces the other (`handlesBetween`), and its arrow points along it. The
  graph reports one flow for all its edges (`data-a2a-flow`: asking before
  answering before still) and how many members it draws
  (`data-a2a-members`).

### How a tool call reaches the graph

Accounting's runtime tells each tool call over A2A, as it happens. Its
pydantic-ai adapter streams every tool call and every tool call's end, among
the text. The A2A worker publishes each one as a `working` status whose
message has one data part: `{tool_call: {id, name, arguments}}` when the
call starts, then `{tool_result: {id, name, result, error}}` when it ends.
In the browser, `askA2APeer` reads them into the `working` event's `tool`
(`{id, name, ended}`), beside its `note`. `callsAfter` keeps a call from its
start to its end, matched by its id. A new request, an answer or a failure
ends them all.

Datalayer's home page runs the same team for signed-out visitors: Sales in
their browser with their trial key, Accounting on a cloud runtime open to
visitors.

## The Agent Inspector

The Agent Inspector shows what agents do as OpenTelemetry spans, the same
signals the runtime sends to the OTEL service, recorded in the page as they
happen and drawn by Datalayer core's OTEL view. A model turn, a tool call
(MCP, frontend, skill, codemode or runtime tool) and an A2A request are each
a span; what a peer answers arrives as events on the request's span. It is
reusable: any page, chat or team can feed it (`components/inspector`).

Each turn is a trace, newest last, with its tool calls and A2A requests
under it, unfolded. Each row has:

- the time, to the millisecond;
- the span's name: `invoke_agent Sales`, `execute_tool list_invoices`,
  `a2a SendStreamingMessage`, `a2a agent card`;
- the tool's mark (`SpecMark`), *running* while the span is open, *ERROR*
  when it failed, and the tokens of a turn;
- a line that says it: a call's arguments, a turn's question and answer, a
  request's words and its task's state;
- the agent that did it (the span's service) and the duration.

Click a row for the span's detail: its attributes, with a call's arguments
and result unfolded as JSON, its events (each A2A status update, artifact or
message, whole), the trace tree, and the raw span. The toolbar filters by
source (A2A, MCP, Frontend tool, Skill, Codemode, Runtime tool, Model),
operation and agent, pauses the rows while recording goes on, clears them,
and exports the spans as JSON.

### What is recorded

The spans follow the OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions that the runtime's own
spans follow:

| Span | Attributes |
| --- | --- |
| `invoke_agent <agent>`: a turn | `gen_ai.operation.name`, `gen_ai.agent.name`, `gen_ai.request.model`, `gen_ai.input.messages`, `gen_ai.output.messages`, `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens`, `gen_ai.usage.output_tokens` |
| `execute_tool <tool>`: a tool call, under its turn | `gen_ai.tool.name`, `gen_ai.tool.call.id`, `gen_ai.tool.call.arguments`, `gen_ai.tool.call.result`, `gen_ai.tool.type`; `datalayer.tool.kind` (`mcp`, `frontend`, `skill`, `codemode`, `runtime`) and the tool's marks (`datalayer.mark.icon`, `datalayer.mark.emoji`) |
| `a2a <method>`: an A2A request, a client span of the asker's | `rpc.system` `jsonrpc`, `rpc.service` `a2a`, `rpc.method`, `rpc.jsonrpc.request_id`, `peer.service` (the agent asked), `a2a.request`, `a2a.message.text`, `a2a.task.id`, `a2a.context.id`, `a2a.task.state`, `http.response.status_code`; its events `a2a.task`, `a2a.status_update`, `a2a.artifact_update`, `a2a.message`, `a2a.error` |
| `a2a agent card`: the card fetched | `peer.service`, `a2a.agent.name`, `a2a.agent.skills`, `a2a.agent_card` |

An error is the span's status `ERROR` and an `exception` event. Each agent
is a service: one agent's inspector shows what it did, and the requests sent
to it (`peer.service`).

### Feeding it

```tsx
import {
  AgentInspector,
  traceA2AFetch,
  useAgentInspector,
} from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/components/inspector';

const { tracer } = useAgentInspector();
// The A2A traffic: a fetch under the SDK's JSON-RPC transport.
connectA2APeer({
  url,
  fetch: traceA2AFetch(fetch, { tracer, asker: 'Sales', peer: 'Accounting' }),
});
// The team: the entry's turns and its call to the peer.
useA2ATeam({ ...options, inspector: tracer });
// A chat: its turns and tool calls (ChatBase, ChatFloating, …).
<ChatFloating inspector={tracer} … />;
<AgentInspector tracer={tracer} agent="Sales" compact />;
```

- **`useAgentInspector()`** makes the page's tracer once: core's
  `createOtelLiveTracer`, kept in memory (2000 spans by default, the oldest
  ended ones dropped first; `createOtelLiveTracer({ limit })` for another
  cap). A span started without a parent nests under the innermost span its
  agent still has open, so the A2A request lands under the tool call that
  made it.
- **`traceA2AFetch`** hands each request to the network and reads a copy of
  the answer, so the SDK sees exactly what the network gave. Each JSON-RPC
  request is a client span; the response, or each server-sent event, is an
  event on it, with its task id, context id and state. It never records the
  bearer key. Each `{tool_call}` / `{tool_result}` pair a peer tells on its
  `working` statuses becomes an `execute_tool` span of the peer's, under the
  request (`datalayer.span.origin` `a2a.status`): an MCP call when it is one
  of the peer's connections (`teamToolClassifier`), otherwise a tool its
  runtime runs. The chunks of one artifact are one event.
- **`ChatBase`** records into its `inspector` prop, or into the tracer of the
  nearest `AgentInspectorProvider`: each turn and each tool call from start
  to end, its kind by who claims the tool's name (`classifyToolCall`).
- **`<AgentInspector agent="Sales" />`** shows one agent's own spans. One A2A
  request shows in both agents' inspectors. `compact` narrows it for a panel;
  without it, the detail opens beside the list.
- **`AgentInspectorDialog`** shows it over the page. The assistant's menu
  opens it with *Inspect the agent…*, and loads it only then.

### The duplicated tool lines

The old exchange list showed `Accounting: Calling odoo_accounting_list_invoices`
twice, then `Read …` twice. These were not duplicated events. Recorded on
the wire, each `working` status came once, and each call had its own id. The
model called the tool twice at once, with different arguments
(`payment_state=not_paid` and `payment_state=partial`), and the line named
neither. The inspector shows each call as its own span, with its arguments
and its duration.

- **Sales' inspector** shows its model turns, its `ask_accounting` call, and

## The Scenes example

`ScenesExample` (the Apps group) puts the catalogue's scenes in tabs
(`listSceneSpecs`), each its face and its name. The tab chosen is drawn from

It plays against the local servers, so a scene is tried before it is
deployed anywhere. The entry in the browser asks its model through the

served it, and the others on a runtime each from :8768 on, in the

## A temporary key

`examples/sales-accounting-a2a/make_temp_key.py --runtime <uid> --url <A2A url>`
mints a key with IAM's task grants and writes it to `.env.local` as
`VITE_A2A_ACCOUNTING_KEY`. That file is ignored by git, and the key is never
printed. The key reaches:

- that one runtime's Accounting A2A route (its task
  `a2a:<runtime uid>:accounting:<nonce>`);
- the Datalayer MCP gateway in the owner's name, read only, through the
  `odoo_accounting_*` tools whose classes are all `read` (one
  `datalayer_connection` detail, scope `data:read`);
- for `--hours` (four by default, at most 24).

It reaches nothing else. The script shows the grant and waits for a yes. It
needs a platform service key to mint, and `--revoke <grant uid>` ends the
key early.
