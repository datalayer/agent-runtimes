# What an application remembers

An application remembers when its Appspec says so:

```yaml
memory: mem0
```

`mem0` is the memory of the catalogue enabled today. Any other is not enabled,
and the application's setup notes say so; without `memory`, it remembers
nothing between conversations. Its **agent's** memory setting does not apply:
an application has what its Appspec names.

## Per person and application

What it learns is kept in mem0 under two keys:

- **the person**: whoever opened the conversation — the runtime's owner, the
  trusted identity the runtime is launched with (`DATALAYER_USER_UID`), or a
  visitor signed in, under the uid of the token the platform verified for
  them; never anything a caller says of themselves;
- **the application**: `app:<its uid>`, the `app` item the platform keeps it
  as — or `app:<its id>` for an application run from its file, which has none.

Its Preview and every deployment of it remember together: a **deployment has
no memories of its own**. That is why a deployment's *Reset* leaves them, and
says where they are forgotten.

On Datalayer the store is the shared pgvector database every runtime of the
person reaches; on a laptop, a FAISS index on disk
(`AGENT_RUNTIMES_MEM0_DIR`).

## Each person apart

An application remembers each person apart: a visitor at its address is
remembered under their own uid, separately from its owner and from every other
visitor, and reads only what it learned of them. Each of them reads, corrects
and forgets their own (the routes below, or the runtimes service).

Nobody is never its owner. A visitor who is not signed in — or one in an
embed, which does not know who its visitor is — has nothing remembered and
nothing read: the memory tools answer in a sentence, *Nothing is remembered in
this conversation: its visitor is not signed in…*. A run outside a session —
the scheduler waking a worker, as its owner — remembers as its owner.

## Shared with the applications a person allows

An application's agent reads what it remembers of the person, and — only when
that person allows it — what other applications remember of them. It never
reads another application's memories otherwise, and it writes what it learns
under its own key only. A memory read from another application is marked
with the application that remembered it (`remembered_by`), after its own.

The allowances are the person's, kept by the runtimes service beside the
memories, one per person and pair of applications: *`reader` may use what
`source` remembers of me*. The runtime reads them once per turn, with the
caller's token, at `DATALAYER_RUNTIMES_URL`; a turn without a token (the
machine itself, the scheduler's tick) reads its own only. A runtime with a
token but no `DATALAYER_RUNTIMES_URL`, or a service that refuses, reads
nothing that turn and says why in its log: it never guesses what was allowed.

| Route of the runtimes service | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/runtimes/v1/memory-shares?source=app:<uid>` | Who reads what the application remembers of the caller: `{shares: [{source, reader, allowed_at}]}`. With `?reader=app:<uid>`, what the application reads besides its own. |
| `PUT /api/runtimes/v1/memory-shares` | Allow: `{source, reader}`, both `app:<uid>`; allowed again, kept as it was. An application is refused as its own reader (422). |
| `DELETE /api/runtimes/v1/memory-shares?source=…&reader=…` | Stop sharing; what was never allowed is not found (404). |

The TypeScript client has them as `listRuntimeMemoryShares`,
`allowRuntimeMemoryShare` and `stopRuntimeMemoryShare`, and on
`AgentRuntimesClient`.

## The routes

On the runtime, under `/api/v1/apps`, for the person calling — what the
application remembers of **them**: its owner (or the machine itself), or a
visitor signed in. A caller who is not a person signed in is refused (401,
403).

| Route | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /memories/{app}` | What it remembers, newest first: `{app, count, memories: [{id, memory, created_at, updated_at, corrected_by, corrected_at}]}`. `?limit=` up to 1000. |
| `PATCH /memories/{app}/{memory_id}` | **Correct**: one thing in place, with `{memory}` the words it should remember. mem0 embeds them again; the correction is kept with who made it and when (`corrected_by`, `corrected_at`); when it was learned stays. Empty words, or more than 2000 characters, are refused (422); another application's, or somebody else's, is not found (404). Answers `{corrected, memory}`. |
| `DELETE /memories/{app}/{memory_id}` | **Forget this**: one thing. Another application's, or somebody else's, is not found (404). |
| `DELETE /memories/{app}?count=N` | **Forget everything** it remembers of the caller, no more than the `N` they were shown and confirmed: when it remembers more now, nothing is forgotten (409). Answers `{forgotten}`. |

`{app}` is its uid, or its id when it runs from its file. A runtime without
mem0 installed answers 503 in a sentence.

## Without a runtime

On Datalayer, the runtimes service reads the same store for the person, so an
application's page shows what it remembers whether or not a runtime is running:

| Route of the runtimes service | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/runtimes/v1/memories?agent_id=app:<uid>` | The caller's memories of the application. |
| `PATCH /api/runtimes/v1/memories/{memory_id}` | Correct one of the caller's own in place, `{memory}` its new words, kept with `corrected_by` and `corrected_at`; somebody else's is not found. The service has no embedder: the memory keeps the vector of its old words, and is found by meaning as it was until it is learned again. Its words, and the words it is found by keyword, are the new ones. |
| `DELETE /api/runtimes/v1/memories/{memory_id}` | Forget one of the caller's own; somebody else's is not found. |
| `DELETE /api/runtimes/v1/memories?agent_id=app:<uid>&count=N` | Forget everything the application remembers of the caller, no more than `N` (409 otherwise). |

The TypeScript client has them as `listRuntimeMemories`,
`correctRuntimeMemory`, `forgetRuntimeMemory` and `forgetRuntimeMemories`
(`@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/api/runtimes/runtimes`), and on
`AgentRuntimesClient`.
