# The floating assistant

A chat can live on the page as a **character**, after the Office Assistant:
it sits in a corner, idle and alive, acts out what the agent is doing, and
says the agent's words in a speech balloon. Click it and the conversation
opens in a balloon beside it; drag it and it moves, the conversation
following; send it away and it leaves a way back.

It is the fifth way `ChatFloating` shows a chat, and needs nothing from the
page, as the other floating modes do.

## The five display modes

The header of an open chat offers the modes as a segmented toggle, in this
order (`CHAT_VIEW_MODES`, in `chat/viewModes.ts`):

| Mode | Label | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `floating` | Full-height popup | A panel pinned to the edge of the window, full height. |
| `floating-small` | Floating popup | A popup over a round button in a corner. |
| `floating-draggable` | Floating draggable | The popup with a handle, moved about the window. |
| `assistant` | Floating assistant | A character in a corner that speaks in a balloon (a paper clip in the toggle). |
| `sidebar` | Sidebar panel | The chat docked into the page. |

The four floating modes need nothing from the page. *Sidebar panel* has to
dock somewhere: the host passes `sidebarMountPoint` (an element, or a
selector for one), and without it the option is shown greyed out, with the
tooltip *No mount point for a sidebar on this page*.

On a narrow screen (under 640 pixels) the character is not drawn: the round
button of the floating popup stands in its place.

## The character and its motions

The character acts out the state of what answers — the same state an
application's [presence](./presence.mdx) says in its line of status — plus
three moments of its own. One set of motions serves every character
(`chat/assistant/state.ts`, `AssistantStage.tsx`):

| State | When | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `idle` | Between turns | Floats gently and blinks now and then. |
| `thinking` | A turn runs and no tool is open | Tilts its head, eyes up. |
| `working` | A tool has not returned | A quick sway, eyes down to the work. |
| `waiting` | A tool waits on the person's approval | Calls for attention; the balloon holds the approval, with *Approve* and *Deny*. |
| `paused` | Its owner paused its deployment | Dozes: its eyes shut, its breath slow, a little greyed; the balloon says it is paused. |
| `greeting` | It has just appeared | Two hops on arrival. |
| `speaking` | The answer is being written | Its mouth moves while the words arrive. |
| `goodbye` | It is being sent away | Shrinks away. |

The state is read from the chat itself — whether a turn is running and the
newest tool call — never from the model's words (`assistantStateOf`); paused
is the deployment's, said by its host. Going wins, being short; then
*paused* — a paused application neither greets nor speaks; then arriving;
then *waiting*, which must never be missed; then *speaking* over *thinking*.

Nothing moves for a reader whose system asks for reduced motion
(`prefers-reduced-motion`): each state stands as one still frame — paused,
its eyes shut. A character one brings dozes with its own snooze or sleep
animation when it has one (`IdleSnooze`, `Sleep`, `Rest`…), else idles.

## The speech balloon

**A click on the character toggles the conversation**: closed, it opens; open,
it closes. A press that moves is a drag, not a click.

### Closed: a peek

While the conversation is closed, **what the agent has to say peeks from a
small balloon over the character**, without opening the conversation: one
short line and the actions it needs. A click on the line opens the
conversation, where it is.

- **A new message**: the agent's newest words, as the Office Assistant said
  them (`latestSaying`) — only its own messages with text, not the person's
  words nor a tool call, Markdown read as plain words — cut to their first
  words (60 characters, at a word, `peekLine`). It shows while the words
  arrive and for 12 seconds after, and on a hover until the conversation has
  been opened — opening it counts the words as read; its × puts it away.
- **An approval** the agent waits on is answered in the peek (T-23):
  *Waiting for you*, what it asks to do, the rule it was asked under,
  **Approve** and **Deny**, and how many more wait (*2 more*). It has no ×:
  it stays until it is answered, there or in the conversation. The answer goes
  where every approval of the application is answered (the Tool Approvals
  page, its rules card, its page, the channels): over the ai-agents `/ws`,
  where each decision is broadcast, so an approval answered in the balloon
  leaves every list. In a LOOP workspace the assistant reads them as the
  rules card does, by the application's id (`appId` in its config) and only
  signed in; the chat's own floating assistant answers with the chat's own
  `pendingApprovals`, `onApproveApproval` and `onRejectApproval`. Without
  them, the balloon says *Waiting for you — open the conversation to
  answer.*;
- **A notification**: while its deployment is paused, the peek says
  *Paused — it answers again once it is resumed.*

With nothing new to say, a hover shows the chat's `description` — its
welcome. The peek stays inside the window: it opens toward the middle of
the page from whichever half the character stands in, and below the
character when it stands near the top (`balloonSide`).

### Open: the conversation as a balloon

Open, **the balloon is the conversation itself**, over the character
wherever it was put down, its tail toward it — above it, below it near the
top of the window, beside it when the window is too short for either:

- the history, drawn as the chat draws its messages, **the welcome first**
  (the chat's `description`, never sent nor kept), and an approval waiting
  as a message with **Approve** and **Deny**;
- **the composer last**, its Lexical variant;
- **a header line that counts it** — *Conversation · 6*, the messages of the
  person and the agent, not the tool calls — and nothing else on top;
- **no footer**, and none of the session's controls
  (agents, model, tools, skills, context), no turn footers, no *Powered by*;
- **a close control** in its corner (×) — closing keeps the conversation,
  and Escape closes it too;
- 380px wide and **60% of the window tall, 560px at most**: the history
  scrolls inside it, kept on the newest message, and the composer stays in
  sight at the bottom;
- *Ask a decision* beside the composer, when it is given a runtime to ask
  (below).

That is the `history` display. The other one is `current`.

### History or current

**How the balloon shows the conversation is a setting of its own**,
`balloonDisplay` on `ChatFloating` and `AssistantStage`, `balloon` in the
LOOP assistant plugin's config, and `interface.balloon` in an application's
Appspec (`history` or `current`; the page's own when unsaid):

| | `history` | `current` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closed | every message of the conversation, the person's and the agent's, under *Conversation · 6*, scrolled to the newest (a peek of the newest words when the host gives no messages) | **Now**, then what is said or done now — the answer as it is written, whole (four lines at most, nothing to scroll), or the tool it calls |
| Open | the whole conversation under *Conversation · 6*, scrolled, the composer last | that one line over the composer, 230px tall, no header, no history |
| Looks | the plain balloon, the theme's border | a compact speech bubble edged with the theme's accent, *Now* with a dot that breathes while the agent works (still under reduced motion) |
| Default for | the floating chat (`ChatFloating`), the LOOP workspace's assistant | a team's members (`A2ATeamGraph`) |

**The balloon draws with the chat's own components**: its history is the
chat's items as the chat holds them (`DisplayItem`s: messages and tool
calls), listed by `ChatMessageList` in its `compact` density (no avatars,
smaller bubbles and tool rows); the agent's words are drawn by the chat's
markdown (`ChatMarkdown`: lists, code blocks, tables), the tool calls by
the chat's tool card (`ToolCallDisplay`, with its approvals), and the three
dots are the chat's. Drawn large (*Expand*), it is the same list in the
chat's own density: closed with the dialog's ×, or Esc; in a page area,
with its *Shrink* icon.

```tsx
<ChatFloating defaultViewMode="assistant" balloonDisplay="current" … />
```

```yaml
interface:
  assistant: wizard
  balloon: current   # or history
```

In `current` the balloon replaces its content as the turn goes on —
*Thinking…*, then the tool, then the words — and the words are read out
once they have all arrived (`aria-busy` while they stream), not at every
token. An application embedded as an assistant takes `interface.balloon`;
its conversation, drawn by the page that hosts it, opens as it is drawn
there.

### A large visual in the balloon

What goes with the words — a notebook given today, a chart or a table later
— shows **in either display, and while a tool line is up**: compact in the
balloon (`balloon.attachment`, a notebook's read-only preview). When it is
the compact form of **a large visual** (`balloon.visual`: an id, a title,
and how it is drawn large), the balloon offers *Expand*, and a click on the
compact form does the same:

- with **`expandTarget`** (on `AssistantStage`, `ChatFloating` and a
  team's members), a ref to an element of the page, the visual is drawn
  there, through a portal, scrolled to and focused, with a close control;
  `expandOnArrival` draws it there as soon as it arrives;
- **without one**, it opens in a dialog over the page, about 80% of the
  window wide and tall, in the theme's colours; the focus stays in it, and
  Esc closes it.

The expanded visual stays while the balloon moves on to other words, until
it is closed or another is expanded. **A notebook's large view is the one
that runs**: `notebookBalloonVisual(notebook, title)` draws `TeamNotebook`,
editable, on the browser sandbox (a Pyodide kernel in the page), loaded only
once it is expanded.

```tsx
const area = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
<AssistantStage
  balloon={{
    text: 'Here is the notebook.',
    attachment: <NotebookPreview notebook={nb.data} title="The notebook" />,
    visual: notebookBalloonVisual(nb, 'The notebook'),
  }}
  expandTarget={area}   // leave out for the dialog
  …
/>
<div ref={area} />
```

### Tool calls in the balloon

**When the agent calls a tool, the balloon shows it as the chat does**: the
chat's tool card, compact, *Executing…* while it runs, then *Complete* or
*Failed*; a screen reader hears it in plain words, once as it starts and
once as it ends: *Using **list_invoices**…*, *Done: list_invoices*,
*list_invoices failed*. The name is the one the tool's spec displays when it
has one (`Current Time` for `current_time`), else its name without its MCP
server's prefix; the mark beside it is the catalogue's (its MCP server's,
skill's or tool set's, `SpecMark`), a tool otherwise. Where the runtime says
what a tool does in its own words, those are its card's summary (*Writing a
notebook…*, *Asking Accounting…*); a LOOP turn's `activity`, which names no
tool, is the agent's message saying it.

- **Closed**, in either display, the peek is the tool line while the call
  runs and while its turn goes on — the person sees what the agent is doing
  without opening it;
- **open in `current`**, it replaces the line said now;
- **open in `history`**, the call is a compact line of the conversation, as
  the chat draws every tool call (`ToolCallDisplay`).

A screen reader hears a tool line **once when the call starts and once when
it ends**, politely, from a live region of its own — never while it runs,
nor at every redraw (`toolAnnouncement`).

The lines are read from the chat's items (`newestToolLine`: the newest item,
when it is a tool call), from a LOOP turn's `activity`, or, in a team, from
the steps a peer tells over A2A (`toolLineOfStep`).

### In a team

A team's members (`A2ATeamGraph`) speak in `current` balloons: Sales says
*Asking Accounting…* while its `ask_accounting` call runs, and Accounting
*Using list_invoices…* while it calls Odoo, from the tool events A2A carries.
When Accounting gives a notebook, **Sales' balloon shows it read-only**:
jupyter-react's `Notebook` with `readonly`, on `ServiceManagerLess` — no
kernel, no Pyodide, nothing to run or edit — loaded only when a notebook
arrives (`NotebookPreview`), scrolled within the balloon, with *Open it below
to run it* and *Expand*. The notebook that runs (`TeamNotebook`, on the
browser sandbox) is the balloon's large visual, drawn into the area the page
names under the graph (`expandTarget` on the member) as it arrives; a click
in the balloon scrolls to it and focuses it. Without an area, *Expand* opens
it in a dialog.

### Suggestions in the balloon

While the agent waits for a question (idle, or greeting), the balloon offers
its **suggestions as chips** under its words, in either display. Click one
to send it as the prompt, the same as typing it and pressing *Send*. Each
chip is a button whose accessible name is the prompt it sends (*Ask: Which
customer invoices are still open?*). The chips go away while the agent
works.

```tsx
<AssistantStage
  suggestions={[{ label: 'Open invoices', prompt: 'Which invoices are open?' }]}
  onSuggestion={suggestion => send(suggestion.prompt)}
  …
/>
```

`ChatFloating` offers its chat's `suggestions` this way and sends them to
its chat. A team's member takes `suggestions` and `onSuggestion`: the team
example and the home page put Sales' starters there. The row of buttons under
the graph is gone, and the row keeps only the trial key's clock.

### While it works: three dots

While the agent thinks, works or waits and has written nothing yet, the
balloon shows the chat's own **three dots** (`TypingDots`, the same
component the conversation uses). In `current` they follow the line said now
(*Thinking*, *Using list_invoices*, *Asking Accounting*), or stand alone
when there is no line. In `history` they are the last item. They stop when
the answer's words stream in, or when the turn ends. They hold still, but
stay visible, for a reader who asks for reduced motion, and a screen reader
does not hear them: the balloon's live region already says the state.

### More, less, and the history large

When the words in a `current` balloon are cut, by the four lines it shows or
because the host shortened them (a team member's `saying`, its uncut text in
`fullText`), a **more** button follows them. It shows the whole text in the
balloon, taller and scrolled, and **less** folds it back. It is a button
with `aria-expanded`, and it shows only when something was cut.

In `history`, the header's **Expand** button draws the whole history large:
every message, the tool line and what goes with the words. It opens in the
balloon's large-visual dialog, or in the host's `expandTarget` when there is
one. **Shrink** (or Esc) puts it back in the balloon.

## The assistant's menu

Right-click the character, press Shift+F10 or the ContextMenu key while it
has the focus, or long-press it on a touch screen. A menu opens where the
pointer is, and Esc or a click outside closes it. Each host shows only the
entries it supports:

| Entry | Shown when the host passes |
| --- | --- |
| Inspect the agent… | `inspector`: the Agent Inspector in a large dialog, loaded only then |
| Agent Details… | `about`: the agent's `AgentDetails` in a dialog, loaded only then. On a runtime (`about.agentId` and `about.apiBase`; the floating chat fills them from its `endpoint`) it reads the spec, MCP servers and codemode there; an agent in the page shows its spec, model, description and where it runs |
| Code Sandbox Details… | `sandbox`: where the agent's code runs (Pyodide in the page, a local or remote Jupyter server, a Datalayer runtime, the runtime's own `eval`), its URL without tokens, its kernel, and its variables (jupyter-react's `KernelVariables`, with its renderers; ipywidgets live where the page talks to the kernel). The floating chat takes it from the runtime's codemode status, the Loop from its sandbox, a team's entry from the notebook open under the graph |
| Open / Close the conversation | always, unless `conversationLabel={false}`; a host may rename it (*Ask Sales*) |
| Stop the turn | `onStop`, while the agent works |
| New conversation, Clear the conversation | `onNewChat`, `onClear` |
| Suggestions | `suggestions` and `onSuggestion` |
| Balloon: History / Current | `onBalloonDisplayChange`; the radio shows the display in use |
| Change character… | `onChangeCharacter` |
| Mute / Unmute speech | `speech` |
| Reset position | `onResetPosition`, after a drag |
| About this agent | `about`: its name, spec, model and where it runs |
| Send away | always: for now, for this session, or for good, as the hover × does |

A host or a plugin adds its own entries with `contextMenu`
(`{id, label, onSelect, checked?, disabled?, group?}`). In the LOOP
workspace, plugins contribute them to `loop.assistant.menu`
(`LoopAssistantMenu`).

`ChatFloating` keeps the balloon display chosen in the menu, and tells the
host with `onBalloonDisplayChange`. It records its agent into `inspector`,
stops and starts its chat, and mutes its voice. A team's members keep
their own display choice, and their history lists what each was asked,
said and did.

## Ask a decision

Given a runtime to ask, **the balloon offers *Ask a decision***: a typed
decision asked of Jev, the typed-decision model
(`cloudflare:wrk/typesafe/jev`), without going through the agent — beside the
composer in the open conversation; in a LOOP workspace, whose conversation
is the workspace's own, in the character's peek. It opens a small form:

- **Situation**: the text the question is about — a ticket, a message, a
  review;
- **Question**: a statement to hold or not, or a question;
- **Type**: *Yes or no*; *A choice*, with its options listed one per line (at
  least two); *A score*, with its range, from 1 to 5 by default;
- **Name**: what the answer is said under (*Answer* when left empty).

*Ask* sends it to the runtime's `POST /api/v1/configure/inference/decisions`,
the route that asks as the agent's `decide` tool does, through ai-inference,
with the token the runtime calls its models with — so the token path is the
same as the tool's. The answer comes back in the balloon, in plain words with
its confidence:

| Type | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Yes or no | `Urgent: yes (0.87)` — a *no* is said with the probability that the statement does not hold |
| A choice | `Team: Billing (0.94)` |
| A score | `Score: 4 of 5 (0.5)` on a range of numbers; `Severity: blocking (0.7)` on a rubric of words |

When nothing was decided, the runtime's sentence is said instead (*Nothing was
decided: …*). While the form or its answer is on screen, the balloon stays,
hovered or not; *Cancel* (or Escape) puts it away, *Ask another* clears the
answer.

The floating assistant of a chat takes the runtime as `decisions`; in a LOOP
workspace, `LoopAssistantPlugin` takes it in its config. Without it, the
balloon does not offer to ask:

```tsx
<ChatFloating
  defaultViewMode="assistant"
  decisions={{ serverUrl: 'http://localhost:8765' }}
  {...chatProps}
/>

configurePlugin(LoopAssistantPlugin, {
  app: app.interface.assistant,
  decisions: { serverUrl: runtimeUrl, token },
});
```

`token` is sent as a bearer when the runtime needs one; the machine's own
server does not. See [The decide tool](../cli#the-decide-tool) for
`/decisions`, the same route from the terminal.

### The Decide example

`AgentDecideExample` (`src/examples/AgentDecideExample.tsx`) draws the
*Decide* application of the catalogue (agentspecs `apps/decide.yaml`: the
agent `example-simple` with the `decide` tool, instructions to answer by
asking a typed decision, decision questions as its starters, and one rule —
deciding is a read, done without asking) in the LOOP workspace on the Local
target, with the floating assistant beside it. Ask in the chat (*Is this
ticket urgent? 'Help! My payouts have been failing for 3 days.'*) and the agent
calls `decide`; or hover the character and *Ask a decision* from its balloon.

It needs a local agent-runtimes server that reaches ai-inference. Start it
with the key of your Datalayer account, never printed nor committed:

```bash
AGENT_RUNTIMES_INFERENCE_PROVIDER_OVERRIDE=datalayer \
DATALAYER_AI_INFERENCE_URL=https://r1.datalayer.run \
DATALAYER_AI_INFERENCE_API_KEY="$DATALAYER_API_KEY" \
  python -m agent_runtimes serve --port 8765
```

then the examples (`npm run examples:vite`, on port 3000), and pick
*AgentDecideExample* in their selector; it needs no sign-in. A server on
another port is named in the page's address:
`http://localhost:3000/?agentRuntimesUrl=http://localhost:<port>`.

## The floating popup speaks too

The same reading of the agent's words serves the plain *Floating popup*.
Closed, its round button **blinks while the agent has said something not yet
read** — a soft pulse of the accent, with a ring going out — and **hovered,
it shows those words in the same balloon**, with *Open the conversation*.
Once the chat is opened the words count as read and the blinking stops.
Under reduced motion both stand still: no blink, and the ring is held around
the button instead of going out.

## Sending it away, and calling it back

Hovering the character shows a small control in its corner. Its menu sends
the assistant away for as long as the person says:

| Choice | How long | Kept in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Hide for now* | This page | Nothing: a reload brings it back |
| *Hide for this session* | Until the browser session ends | The session's storage |
| *Don't show again* | For good | The browser's local storage |

It says goodbye, and the popup's round button takes its place. Clicked, that
button **calls it back** when it went *for now*, and **opens the chat**
otherwise. Choosing *Floating assistant* in the header's toggle always calls
it back, however long it went for, and forgets the choice.

A press that moves is a drag, not a click: dragging the character never
opens the conversation.

## Out of the way

The character never stands over something the person needs:

- **an open dialog or overlay** — a Primer `Dialog`, an `ActionMenu` or
  `SelectPanel` overlay, a native `<dialog>`, anything with the role
  `dialog` or `alertdialog`;
- **another chat's composer** — the box one types in, in any chat of the
  page (its own conversation's composer does not count);
- **the pointer at work** — a press, or a drag, over it or within 24 pixels
  of it, outside the character and its own conversation.

It **steps aside**: it fades out, leaves the tab order and lets the pointer
through to what was under it. It comes back once the dialog or the composer
is no longer under it, and once the pointer has been away from it for a
second and a half — a pointer still moving there keeps it aside, so it does
not reappear under the pointer. A pointer only passing by does not move it,
and its own menu, open, puts nothing aside. The page is looked over every
400 milliseconds (`useKeepClear`, in `AssistantStage.tsx`; the rules in
`state.ts`).

## Pictured

Each state of the paper clip — idle, thinking, working, waiting for you (an
approval peeking, with *Approve* and *Deny* and *1 more*), paused (its
notification peeking), speaking (a new message peeking by its first words) —
the paper clip stepped aside for a dialog, and the conversation open as its
balloon (`assistant-open`: *Conversation · 2* over the history, the welcome
first, the Lexical composer last, no footer) and the `current` balloon saying
the tool it calls (`assistant-current`: *Now*, *Using list_invoices…*) are
pictures, in the light and the dark mode, that `npm run test:pictures`
takes in Chrome and compares with the ones kept in `pictures/baselines/`. A
change in how the assistant is drawn fails that run with a picture of the
difference; see *Pictures as tests* in `TESTING.md` to accept it. So are the
wizard, the cat, the L👀P eyes and a character contributed by a plugin (the
owl below), each idle in its light and its dark drawing:
`npm run test:pictures -- -g "assistant|character"` takes only these.
And the gallery's grid below, every character in every state, is one picture
per mode (`assistant-gallery-light`, `assistant-gallery-dark`).

Every cell is the real `AssistantStage`; nothing in the gallery is drawn for
it.

## Datalayer's characters

Four characters are drawn for this assistant and owned by Datalayer
(`ASSISTANT_CHARACTERS`, in `chat/assistant/characters.tsx`):

| Id | Name |
| --- | --- |
| `paperclip` | Paper clip (the default) |
| `wizard` | Wizard |
| `cat` | Cat |
| `eyes` | L👀P eyes |

Each is an SVG drawing that names its moving parts — `assistant-body`,
`assistant-pupils`, `assistant-lids`, `assistant-mouth` — and the stage
animates those parts by state, so every character has every motion. An id
that is not one of these is refused with the list of those that are.

### In dark mode

Each character has a drawing of its own for the dark page, not the light
drawing on black. The stage reads the chat's colour mode from the theme
(Primer's `colorScheme`) and draws the matching one (`Drawing({ size,
mode })`, `mode` being `'light'` or `'dark'`). The dark drawings take the
`loop` theme's dark tokens: lines that were ink become the dark mode's ink
(`loopColors.inkDark`), the paper clip's steel and the wizard's violets go
lighter, the cat's whiskers turn light and its outline gives way to its fur,
and the L👀P eyes keep their whites with a near-black pupil (`loopColors.black`)
and a grey lid so a blink still shows.

Both drawings are tested for contrast as the theme's own faces are
(`assistant-characters-contrast.test.tsx`, after primer-addons'
`loopContrast.test.ts`): each character names its key colours by mode
(`keyColours`) — every colour along its edge, and its eyes — and the test
holds them at **3 to 1**, WCAG's threshold for graphics and the parts of an
interface one must see (SC 1.4.11, non-text contrast; 4.5 to 1 is for body
text), against the page's canvas and every accent's stage, light and dark: the
edge against the page, the pupil against the white of the eye, and the eye
against what it sits on (the page, or the face). It also checks the drawing
uses those colours. The light paper clip's wire was darkened to pass it
(`#9AA0AA`, 2.2 to 1 on white, to `#7A808A`), and the light cat got an
outline.

## A character from a plugin

The characters are a contribution point, `loop.assistant.character`
(`LoopAssistantCharacter` in `loop/core`): the catalogue is what the enabled
plugins contribute and nothing else. Datalayer's four come from their own
plugin (`AssistantCharactersPlugin`, `loop/plugins/assistant-characters`);
disabled, they leave the catalogue. A plugin contributes a character — a
drawing, as Datalayer's are, or a character read from a file — under an id:

```tsx
import { contribution, definePlugin } from '@datalayer/reactor';
import { LoopAssistantCharacter } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/loop/core';
import type { AssistantCharacter } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/chat/assistant/characters';

const OWL: AssistantCharacter = {
  id: 'owl',
  name: 'Owl',
  // An SVG naming the parts the stage animates: assistant-body,
  // assistant-pupils, assistant-lids, assistant-mouth; `mode` is the chat's.
  Drawing: ({ size, mode = 'light' }) => <svg width={size} height={size}>…</svg>,
};

export const OwlCharacterPlugin = definePlugin({
  name: '@datalayer/example-plugin-owl-character',
  contributes: [
    contribution(LoopAssistantCharacter, { id: 'owl', character: OWL }, { id: 'owl' }),
  ],
});
```

A page reads the catalogue from its reactor with `assistantCharactersOf`
and a character by id with `assistantCharacterNamed`, which refuses an id no
enabled plugin contributes with the list of those that are:

```tsx
const reactor = buildReactorFromPlugins([AssistantCharactersPlugin, OwlCharacterPlugin]);
await reactor.start();
assistantCharactersOf(reactor).map(entry => entry.id); // paperclip, wizard, cat, eyes, owl
<ChatFloating defaultViewMode="assistant" assistantCharacter={assistantCharacterNamed(reactor, 'owl')} … />
```

The *Assistant* example does just this: its picker lists what the
enabled plugins contribute, the owl of `src/examples/utils/owlCharacterPlugin.tsx`
among them, and draws the one chosen.

### Named by an application, chosen by a person

An application names its character in its Appspec, by the id a plugin
contributes it under: `interface.assistant: owl`. The spec (agentspecs
0.0.27) checks only the id's shape — lowercase letters and digits, words
joined by a hyphen, at most 64 characters — since which characters exist is
known where the plugins are, not in the spec. A person may choose a character
of their own, and whether the assistant shows at all, in their settings; on
an application's page, **the application's own character wins** over the
person's.

`assistantCharacterFor(contributed, { app, person })` decides, in that order,
and the paper clip when neither says: it returns the id, the character and
who said it (`app`, `person` or `default`), or — when nothing enabled
contributes that id — a sentence saying so, with the ids there are. An unknown
id is never replaced by another character.

### In an embedded application

The embed's assistant mode (`<datalayer-app mode="assistant">`, `AppEmbed`)
decides the same way, with no person's choice — a visitor to another
product has none there: the application's own character, else the paper clip,
looked up with `assistantCharacterFor` in what Datalayer's characters and the
host's plugins contribute (`AppEmbed`'s `plugins`; the element takes none, so
it draws one of the four). An id nothing contributes is said in the element's
place. See [Embedding](../loop/embedding).

### In the LOOP workspace

`LoopAssistantPlugin` (`loop/plugins/assistant`) draws the assistant over a
LOOP workspace — the `loop` page, an application's page, its Preview —
through the workspace's `root` slot, from what the enabled plugins contribute.
It is configured with the application's character and the person's:

```tsx
import { configurePlugin } from '@datalayer/reactor';
import { AssistantCharactersPlugin } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/loop/plugins/assistant-characters';
import { LoopAssistantPlugin } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/loop/plugins/assistant';

<AppRenderer
  app={app}
  plugins={[
    AssistantCharactersPlugin,
    OwlCharacterPlugin,
    configurePlugin(LoopAssistantPlugin, {
      app: app.interface.assistant,
      person: 'cat',
      appId: app.id,
    }),
  ]}
/>
```

It acts out what the application is doing as its chat says it
(`LoopChatTurn`'s `presence`: thinking, working while a tool runs, waiting
for the person, paused when its deployment is, idle) and speaks while the
answer is written; it says the agent's newest words in its balloon while the
conversation is out of sight, holds there the approvals the application
waits on when it is given the application's id (`appId`), answered with
*Approve* or *Deny*,
and is dragged and sent away as the chat's own assistant is. Clicked, it opens
and closes the conversation panel on a page layout, and takes the person to
the composer where the conversation is always on screen. An id nothing
enabled contributes is said where the character would stand. Datalayer's
landing adds it with the person's choice from their settings
(`/settings/appearance`), unless they hid it.

## A character one brings

:::caution The characters of Office are Microsoft's

Clippy, Merlin, Links, Rocky, The Dot and the other characters of Microsoft
Office are Microsoft's. They are not shipped with Agent Runtimes. Load only
a character file you have the right to use.

:::

A person or an organisation can bring a character of their own, in one of
two formats. The readers (`chat/assistant/formats`) run **in the page**: the
file is read there, drawn there, and **sent nowhere**.

- **A Microsoft Agent character** — one `.acs` file:
  `readAcsCharacter(await file.arrayBuffer())`. Its header, character,
  palette, states, animations, frames, branches and sounds are read, its
  images decompressed and composited into a sprite sheet on a canvas.
- **A clippy.js character** — its `agent.js`, its map image (`map.png`), and
  a `sounds-mp3.js` or `sounds-ogg.js` if there is one:
  `readClippyCharacter({ agentJs, mapPng, soundsJs })`. The JavaScript is
  read as data and never run: code where data is expected is refused.

Both give one shape, `AssistantCharacterData` — a sprite sheet, frames that
index into it, animations by name — that `SpriteCharacter` plays: frames
stepped as authored, branches taken by their weights, the animation chosen
by the state. Each state above is mapped to the character's own animations
by the names Office characters use (`Thinking`, `Processing`,
`GetAttention`, `Greeting`, `Explain`, `GoodBye`…) and by the states an Agent
file declares (`Showing`, `Speaking`, `Hiding`, `IdlingLevel1`…); a state
with no match plays the idle animations (`stateAnimations`).

The files a person picked are read as one character by `readCharacterFiles`:
one `.acs`, or a clippy.js character's `agent.js` with its map image (and its
sounds file); `characterFilesOf` says which of them make the character, and
`CHARACTER_FILES_ACCEPT` is what a file input should accept. Once read, a
character joins the others as a plugin of its own,
`defineAssistantCharacterPlugin(id, character)`, contributed to
`loop.assistant.character` under the id it is chosen by — that is how
Datalayer's *Settings → Appearance* offers a person their own character on
the pages of their applications, kept in their browser and never sent.

A file that cannot be read is refused with a sentence that says why
(`AssistantCharacterFormatError`). A Microsoft Agent `.acf`, whose
animations live in separate `.aca` files, is refused for now: load the
`.acs` of the character.

## Using it

`ChatFloating` takes two props for the assistant:

| Prop | Type | Default | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `defaultViewMode` | `'floating' \| 'floating-small' \| 'floating-draggable' \| 'assistant' \| 'panel'` | `'floating'` | `'assistant'` opens on the character. |
| `assistantCharacter` | `string \| AssistantCharacter \| AssistantCharacterData` | `'paperclip'` | A Datalayer character by id, a contributed drawing, or one read from a file. |
| `decisions` | `{ serverUrl: string; token?: string }` | — | The runtime its balloon asks typed decisions at: offers *Ask a decision* (see [Ask a decision](#ask-a-decision)). |

The chat's `description` is what the character says on a hover before
anything else has been said.

A host that draws the conversation itself — the Loop workspace, as an
embedded application does — passes it as `conversation`:
`{ body, presence, saying, answering }`. The window holds `body` under its own
header, and the character acts `presence` and says `saying` as it would its
own chat's.

From the *Assistant* example (`src/examples/AssistantExample.tsx`),
with a picker of Datalayer's characters and a file input for a character one
brings (the example itself lists the contributed characters, as above):

```tsx
import { useState } from 'react';
import { ChatFloating } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes';
import { ASSISTANT_CHARACTERS } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/chat/assistant/characters';
import {
  CHARACTER_FILES_ACCEPT,
  readCharacterFiles,
  type AssistantCharacterData,
} from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/chat/assistant/formats';

export function Page() {
  const [character, setCharacter] = useState<string | AssistantCharacterData>(
    ASSISTANT_CHARACTERS[0].id,
  );
  return (
    <>
      {ASSISTANT_CHARACTERS.map(option => (
        <button key={option.id} onClick={() => setCharacter(option.id)}>
          {option.name}
        </button>
      ))}
      <p>
        The characters of Microsoft Office are Microsoft's. Load only a
        character file you have the right to use. It is read in this page and
        sent nowhere.
      </p>
      <input
        type="file"
        multiple
        accept={CHARACTER_FILES_ACCEPT}
        onChange={async event =>
          setCharacter(
            await readCharacterFiles(Array.from(event.target.files ?? [])),
          )
        }
      />
      <ChatFloating
        // A new character is a new stage: it greets again.
        key={typeof character === 'string' ? character : character.sprite}
        defaultViewMode="assistant"
        assistantCharacter={character}
        protocol="vercel-ai"
        endpoint="http://127.0.0.1:8765/api/v1/vercel-ai/assistant"
        title="Assistant"
        description="Hello! Ask me anything about this page."
        position="bottom-right"
        useStore={false}
      />
    </>
  );
}
```

To try it, run the examples app (`npm run examples`) and open
*Assistant*. The character, its balloon and its motions need no account and
no runtime; a conversation needs the local server on port 8765, and says so
when there is none.

## What is verified, and what is not

**Verified.** Unit tests cover the modes and their order
(`viewModes.test.ts`); the state acted out, the balloon's words, the cut at
a word and the away choices (`assistant.test.ts`); the stage in the DOM — the
character drawn, the balloon while closed and never over the open
conversation, a click against a drag, sending it away, where the balloon
goes, stepping aside for a dialog, an overlay, another chat's composer and
the pointer at work, and not for its own conversation
(`assistant-stage.test.tsx`); the clippy.js and `.acs` readers, the
Agent image compression and the mapping of states (`assistant-formats.test.ts`,
on hand-built files); the player's frames and branches
(`sprite-character.test.ts`). Seen in Chrome on 2026-10-04, in the examples
app signed out: the paper clip arrives greeting and settles to idle, a hover
shows the welcome in its balloon, a drag moves it and the conversation opens
above it where it was put down, and the wizard, the cat and the eyes draw.
Each character's dark drawing and its contrast, light and dark, are tested
(`assistant-characters-contrast.test.tsx`), and so is the stage drawing the
chat's mode; a character contributed by a plugin is drawn in the
*Assistant* example and pictured (`character-owl-*.png`). The choice of a
character — the application's over the person's over the paper clip, an
unknown id said and not replaced — and the assistant in a LOOP workspace,
drawn from a plugin's character and acting out the chat's turn, dozing while
paused and holding an approval of its application in its balloon — answered
there, never another application's — are tested (`loop-assistant.test.tsx`;
the chat's own assistant in `chat-floating-conversation.test.tsx`); an Appspec naming a plugin's character is read,
and a wrongly shaped id refused (`app-checks.test.ts`).

**Not verified yet.** A real `.acs` file someone holds the rights to (the
reader is tested on a hand-built file, and the clippy.js reader on the
clippy.js characters, in memory, not kept); the floating popup's blink and
balloon seen in a browser; stepping aside, seen in a browser.

**Not built yet.** Sounds are read but not played; the mouth overlays of an
Agent character while it speaks; `.acf` files; sharing a character one
brought with one's organisation.
