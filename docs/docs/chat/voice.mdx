# Voice

A person can talk to an agent instead of typing, and hear its answers. Voice
is another way into the same conversation, not a second one: what is said
becomes a message, what is heard is the answer the conversation shows, and the
text stays on screen.

- **The microphone in the composer.** Click it and speak: it turns red and
  pressed, and the words are written in the composer **as they are heard**.
  It stops when you click it again, or once you stop speaking (1.5 s of
  silence after speech, found by Silero VAD; `silenceMs` changes it). Or hold
  it, or `Ctrl`+`Space`, and let go. `Esc` cancels and gives the composer
  back what it held. What was said is heard **in the browser**, and the words
  stay in the composer for the person to read, edit and send: nothing is sent
  on its own unless *Send what I say* is on. The sound never leaves the page,
  and is forgotten once heard. No microphone, a refused permission or a model
  that cannot load is said in a sentence beside it.
- **Heard on Datalayer's servers, when asked.** Where the application's voice
  says `where: server`, where it says `auto` and no model in the browser hears
  the language, or where the person chose *Detect the language I speak* in the
  voice menu, the browser still finds the speech and the silence, and sends
  the sound to the speech service as it is captured
  (`WS /api/ai-agents/v1/speech/transcriptions/stream`). There Whisper writes
  it — small for a spoken turn, large-v3-turbo past 20 seconds — finding the
  language itself when asked to detect it. The words heard so far come back
  while the person speaks and are greyed in the box until the final words
  replace them. A press too short for a first look is sent whole, as a
  recording (`POST /api/ai-agents/v1/speech/transcriptions`). The first use asks apart
  for this, in its own sentence: the sound goes to Datalayer's servers, and
  neither it nor the words are kept there. *Keep speech on this device* turns
  all of it off.
- **Talk with it, hands-free.** Where the application's voice says
  `input: hands_free`, *Talk with it* sits beside the microphone. On, the
  microphone stays open: the words are written as they are heard, and once
  you have spoken and been silent for 700 ms (`turnSilenceMs`), what you
  said is heard whole and **sent by itself**. The answer is said as it is
  written (hands-free implies `output: always`), and listening goes on
  through it. What it is doing is shown at all times: *Listening…*,
  *Thinking…*, *Speaking…*, *Paused*. **Speak over it** and its voice stops
  at once; while the answer is still being written, the run is stopped too,
  the answer is marked *interrupted* in the conversation, and what you said
  starts the next turn. `Esc` or *Pause* pauses it; five quiet minutes
  pause it too; the toggle again turns it off.
- **Pauses before I am done.** Someone who pauses longer while speaking
  chooses *Longer* or *Much longer* in the voice menu, or on
  */settings/appearance*: the silence that stops a microphone clicked on
  (1.5 s) and the one that ends a hands-free turn (700 ms) become twice or
  three times as long.
- **A line said, not written.** An agent whose answers may be heard has one
  more tool, `say_aloud`: a short line such as *One moment, I am looking that
  up.*, at most 200 characters. It is shown as a caption beside the composer
  while the run goes on, with a speaker icon, and said where answers are said
  by themselves. The answer itself is always written.
- **Read aloud, on request.** With `output: on_request`, the default of an
  Appspec, each answer the agent has finished has *Read aloud* under it. It
  stops whatever the voice was saying and reads that answer from its start, a
  sentence at a time, as plain words. Nothing is said until it is pressed, so
  a screen reader is never talked over.
- **Spoken answers, always.** With `output: always`, the answer is cut into sentences as it is written. Each
  sentence is said by **Datalayer's speech service**, `datalayer-speech` on r1 (Kokoro, from ai-agents' code) and
  played as it arrives, so the first words are heard before the answer is
  finished. The floating assistant's mouth moves with the sound, and while the
  conversation is closed its balloon shows the sentence being said. `Esc`, or
  *Stop speaking*, stops the voice, and so does opening the microphone.
- **The sentence being said is marked.** While an answer is heard, its
  sentence being said is underlined in the open conversation, and the answer
  has a bar beside it. The mark is not a colour only: the answer is
  `aria-current`, so a screen reader can find where the voice is, and the
  underline follows the voice from sentence to sentence. It goes when the
  voice stops. A browser without the CSS Custom Highlight API keeps the bar
  and `aria-current` without the underline. It works in the chat, in the
  floating assistant and in an application's own composer.
- **What is kept.** A spoken message is sent as text, marked as said
  (`metadata.input: "voice"`, with its language, the model that heard it and
  where it ran). It shows a small microphone in the conversation. An
  application's record keeps the turn as its transcript, marked `spoken`.
  Audio is never kept.

## At the terminal

`loop --voice` listens with the machine's microphone: **`Ctrl`+`T`** (or
`/voice`) starts listening — *Listening… (Ctrl+T or /voice to stop)* — and
the next press stops it and hears what was said; the words go **on the prompt
line**, to edit or send. With `--speak`, each answer is said through the
machine's speaker, by Datalayer's speech service. A terminal sees no key
release, so there is no holding: a press starts, a press stops, and one
press listens a minute at most.

It needs the `voice` extra — `pip install 'agent-runtimes[voice]'`
(sounddevice, MIT) — which is never a default dependency. What is said is
heard **on the machine** when `faster-whisper` is installed beside it and the
pinned store (`DATALAYER_SPEECH_MODELS_DIR`, `~/.cache/voice-work/store` by
default) holds `openai/whisper-small`; otherwise **on Datalayer's servers**
with your token (`DATALAYER_TOKEN` or `DATALAYER_API_KEY`). No microphone, no
extra, no token: voice is refused in a sentence naming what was found, and
the session goes on by typing.

## An application's voice

An Appspec says it under `interface.voice`. Voice is off unless the spec turns
it on:

```yaml
interface:
  voice:
    enabled: true
    input: push_to_talk      # off | push_to_talk | hands_free
    output: always           # off | on_request | always
    voice: kokoro-af-heart   # a voice of the catalogue
    language: en-US          # BCP 47; the person's when unsaid
    where: auto              # auto | device | server: where what is said is heard
```

`POST /api/v1/apps/configure` and `GET /api/v1/apps/current` answer the
voice, resolved against the catalogue, so the page knows it before the first
answer. When a voice doesn't speak the language, the first voice of the
catalogue that does is used, with its attribution when its licence asks for
one. An application whose answers are heard runs with `VoiceCapability`. It
tells the agent that its answers may be heard: short sentences, no table where
a sentence will do, and numbers and links in words. The capability changes no
tool and no rule.

### Who it speaks to

Its voice is asked of the speech service with the token its conversation
already has. A visitor without an account is heard in the browser, and its
answers are asked with the visitor's token from ai-inference: each visitor
hears **120 seconds of spoken answers a day**, recordings heard on the server
counted in the same 120 seconds, and is then told in a sentence to sign in to
hear more. An embedded application's answers are asked with its embed token,
for the deployment **the token names** — the page names none, and a token
naming none is refused. ai-agents checks that the page may open that
deployment, as a runtime does, and counts the speech against the deployment's
spend limit. The owner pays.

`<datalayer-app>` is drawn in the host's page, so the page's
`Permissions-Policy` decides whether the microphone may be asked for. The
snippet from *Ship* says so for an application that listens. When the policy
refuses the microphone, the composer says so in a sentence.

## The voice catalogue

Voices and speech models come from agentspecs (`voices`, `speech-models`) and
are generated into `agent_runtimes.specs.voices` and `src/specs/voices.ts`. A
model or a voice is admitted by the licence of its weights and of its training
data. The register (`speech-licences.yaml`) refuses any licence it doesn't
allow, so non-commercial voices and models never load.

| | Model | Languages | Where | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hearing | `moonshine-tiny-en`, `moonshine-base-en` | English | browser | MIT |
| Hearing | `whisper-base` | French (and English) | browser | MIT |
| Voice activity | `silero-vad` | — | browser | MIT |
| Speaking | `kokoro-82m`: `kokoro-af-heart` (en-US), `kokoro-bf-emma` (en-GB), `kokoro-ff-siwis` (fr-FR, trained on the SIWIS database, CC BY 4.0) | | ai-agents | Apache-2.0 |

There is no speech synthesis in the browser. Every small open voice
phonemizes with eSpeak NG, which is GPL-3.0, and sending it to browsers would
be distributing it. Answers are spoken on the server, where it only runs.

## In a page

`ChatBase`, `ChatFloating` and the workspace's chat (`ChatPlugin`'s
`voice`, which `AppRenderer` sets from an Appspec) take a `voice`. The least
is the microphone alone, heard with the models on Datalayer's CDN:

```tsx
<ChatBase protocol={protocol} voice={{ language: 'en-US', input: 'push_to_talk' }} />
```

Add `output` and `speechUrl` (and the `token` to ask it with) for the answers
to be heard. Everything else has a default: the language's first voice of the
catalogue, `modelsUrl` `https://models-speech.datalayer.tech`, the packages
that hear (`DATALAYER_VOICE_ENGINES`), a silence of 1.5 s. A person's own
settings (`voice/settings`: their language, voice, rate, volume, microphone,
*Send what I say*, speech kept on the device) are laid over it. In full:

```tsx
import { ChatFloating } from '@datalayer/agent-runtimes/lib/chat';

<ChatFloating
  defaultViewMode="assistant"
  protocol="vercel-ai"
  endpoint={`${server}/api/v1/vercel-ai/assistant`}
  voice={{
    language: 'en-US',
    input: 'push_to_talk',
    // `on_request`: *Read aloud* under each answer; `always`: as written.
    output: 'on_request',
    voice: 'kokoro-af-heart',
    // The browser's models, from Datalayer's CDN (the default), each file
    // checked against its SHA-256 before it is read.
    modelsUrl: 'https://models-speech.datalayer.tech',
    // Datalayer's speech service (datalayer-speech, on r1), and the token to ask it with.
    speechUrl: 'https://r1.datalayer.run',
    token,
  }}
/>;
```

An application's page has its voice from its Appspec: `AppRenderer` takes a
`speech` (`{ speechUrl, token }`, where the speech service is) and turns
`interface.voice` into the chat's `voice` (`chatVoiceOf`); a host that gives
no `speech` draws it without a voice. Its consent to the microphone is
remembered under the application's id.

The models are fetched only from `modelsUrl`, laid out as the pinned store is
(`<provider>/<model id>/<path>`, the provider the organisation that published the model: `usefulsensors/moonshine-tiny-en/…`), together with onnxruntime-web's WASM
(`onnxruntime-web/<version>/`, where each build of onnxruntime-web finds the
file it names). A file whose hash isn't the catalogue's is refused; the only
part of a file answered without its check is the first byte transformers.js
asks to learn a file's size (`Range: bytes=0-0`), and any other part is
refused. They are fetched on first use, with the progress shown, and kept in
the browser's cache. Before the browser asks for the microphone the first time,
one sentence says where the audio goes, and the answer is remembered for each
application.

## Run the example

The **Voice chat** example (`VoiceChatExample`) puts the floating assistant on
a page, with push-to-talk and spoken answers in English or French. It needs
three things running on this machine. Each one can be named in the address
instead.

1. **The agent**: `agent-runtimes serve --port 8765 --agent-id
   example-agentic-chat --agent-name assistant --protocol vercel-ai`
   (`?agentRuntimesUrl=`).
2. **The models**: fill the pinned store once, then serve it:

   ```bash
   python scripts/voice/pin_store.py --store ~/.cache/voice-work/store
   python scripts/voice/serve_store.py --store ~/.cache/voice-work/store   # :8770
   ```

   Datalayer's own copy, laid out the same, is
   `https://models-speech.datalayer.tech` (the CDN of
   `s3://datalayer-models-speech`); the speech service copies its models from
   it.

   (`?voiceModelsUrl=`).
3. **The speech service**: Datalayer's, `datalayer-speech` on r1
   (`https://r1.datalayer.run`, the default). It checks the caller's
   Datalayer token, so sign in to the examples. To run it on this machine
   instead, against the same store:

   ```bash
   cd services/ai-agents && pip install -e '.[speech]'
   DATALAYER_SPEECH_MODELS_DIR=~/.cache/voice-work/store \
     uvicorn datalayer_ai_agents.speech.main:app --port 4401
   ```

   and open the example with `?speechUrl=http://127.0.0.1:4401`.

## Measured

`scripts/voice/measure.py` runs the browser's models on the recorded fixtures
(`tests/voice`: LibriSpeech and Multilingual LibriSpeech, CC BY 4.0). It also
has each voice say its sample and a few of the product's sentences, then
transcribes them back. The results go under `tests/voice/measured`.
`test_voice_wer.py` fails any model or voice more than two points of word
error rate above its baseline. Measured on 2026-10-05, with transformers.js on
a CPU (in Node, not the browser):

| | WER | Real-time factor |
|---|---|---|
| `moonshine-tiny-en`, English | 3.3% | 0.03 |
| `moonshine-base-en`, English | 4.9% | 0.05 |
| `whisper-base`, English | 4.1% | 0.16 |
| `whisper-base`, French | 34.1% | 0.11 |
| `kokoro-af-heart`, said and heard back | 18.9% | 0.28 |
| `kokoro-bf-emma`, said and heard back | 16.2% | 0.30 |
| `kokoro-ff-siwis`, said and heard back | 13.6% | 0.24 |

French heard in the browser is weak with Whisper base. On the server,
Whisper (faster-whisper, 8-bit on the CPU) heard the English fixture word for
word. Measured on r1 on 16 October 2026, one pod of 4 CPUs, speech only
(`scripts/speech_bench.py` in services' ai-agents):

| | Time |
|---|---|
| A 9-second turn, told its language (Whisper small) | 4.3–4.6 s |
| The same, detecting its language | 7.2–7.7 s |
| A 3-second turn | 4.2–4.5 s |
| The stream's first partial words (pcm16), words said at 0.7–2.7 s | 5.6 s |
| The stream's final words, after its end | 4.1 s |
| Kokoro's first audio: *One moment.* / a 56-character sentence | 0.9–1.2 s / 2.0–2.3 s |
| 8 turns at once, the last heard | 33 s |

A pod hears one turn at a time, and Whisper's 30-second window costs about
4 seconds whatever the turn's length, so the browser stays the default where
it hears the language.

### Streaming to the server

`WS /api/ai-agents/v1/speech/transcriptions/stream` hears speech as it is
said. The first message says who asks, never the address:
`{"token": "…", "language": "en", "format": "pcm16"}`. Then come binary
frames of 16-bit mono PCM at 16 kHz, or, with `"format": "ogg"`, the pages
of an Ogg Opus stream as a recorder writes them. Every 2 seconds of new sound, what was
said so far is sent back (`{"type": "partial"}`), and the whole at the end
(`{"type": "final", "why"}`): when the page sends `{"type": "end"}`, after
10 seconds of silence, when the principal's seconds are spent, or after
5 minutes. A refusal is `{"type": "refused", "detail"}` in a sentence.

### Over the protocols

On the web the page hears and speaks; AG-UI carries text. Two custom events
say what only the runtime knows: `voice.settings`, right after
`RUN_STARTED`, with the voice and language of an agent whose answers may be
heard, and `voice.say`, the line `say_aloud` says (`{"text", "voice",
"language"}`). The Vercel AI stream carries the same as data parts,
`data-voice.settings` and `data-voice.say`.

A protocol client that sends the sound itself — AG-UI's `AudioInputContent`
in the newest user message, its data inline as base64, WAV, FLAC, Ogg or
WebM — is heard by the speech service with the caller's own token before the
run. The message becomes the transcript, marked spoken, and the sound is not
kept. A source given by address, another type, a missing token or the
service's refusal is answered `422` in a sentence before the run starts.
Recordings sent to `POST /speech/transcriptions` may be WAV, FLAC, Ogg Opus
or WebM Opus (what Chrome's `MediaRecorder` writes), up to a minute.
